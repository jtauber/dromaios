Room for one more.

In binary, 254 is 11111110. Add one and the rightmost bit becomes 1: 11111111. That is 255, and it still fits in eight bits.

Predict it. All eight bits are now on. What do you think the byte will contain after another addition?

The sum needs a ninth bit.

255 + 1 is 256. In binary, that is 1 00000000: a 1 in the next position, worth 256, followed by eight zeroes.

The full answer is correct, but it needs nine bits. When we keep only the rightmost eight, the stored result is 0. This is called wraparound.

The extra bit is the carry out of the byte. Here it is 1. For an addition that fits in eight bits, it is 0. You can reconstruct the full sum as carry × 256 + stored result.

Try it. After wrapping to 0, add one again. Does the carry stay on?

No. This addition is 0 + 1 = 1. It fits in eight bits, so its carry is 0. The display describes the latest addition.

A carry can stay inside the byte.

Try starting at 15: 00001111. Adding one gives 16: 00010000. The addition carries into the next position, but all of its bits still fit inside the byte. The carry out of the whole byte is 0.

Predict it. Start at 127 and add one. Will the carry out of the byte be 0 or 1?

Show an explanation

It is 0. The sum is 128, or 10000000. The leftmost bit is on, but it is still one of the byte’s eight bits.
